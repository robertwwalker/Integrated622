The demo, step by step

These are the prompts used live. Paste each into your own Gemini chat. Think about the question in bold before you open "What we found." Gemini's wording will differ from ours, but the numbers should match.

D1

What's in here?

I've uploaded two files from a fictional nonprofit called Riverbend Community Services. What is in each file? How many rows and columns does each have?

Does the AI's count match the data dictionary?

What we found

staff_quarters has 3,215 rows × 18 columns. site_weeks has 832 rows × 14 columns. Check the AI's numbers against something you know before trusting anything else it says.

D2

What is a row?

What does one row of each file represent? How many different employees are in staff_quarters?
What is the largest commute_miles value, and how many rows have it?

Is staff_quarters 3,215 people? If you averaged commute across rows, who would count more than once?

What we found

One row is one employee in one quarter, and one site in one week. There are only 728 different employees, because a person can appear in up to 8 rows. The longest commute, 108.2 miles, belongs to one person and shows up once for each quarter they were employed. What a row is decides what an average means.

D3

What kind of variable? Then: what's the average engagement?

For each column in staff_quarters, what kind of variable is it?
What is the average engagement score?

Engagement is a 1–5 survey rating. What did the AI assume when it averaged it? What did it do with the blanks? If it gave you more than one average, which one is "the" answer?

What we found

Gemini reported a mean of 3.11 and a median of 3. It noted 1,116 blank rows (35%), and averages leave blanks out. It also offered a second average, 2.80, computed per employee rather than per row.

Both are correct, and they answer different questions: "what is a typical quarter like?" versus "what is a typical employee like?" They differ because people who stay longer and answer the survey more often are more engaged, and they appear in more of the rows being averaged. Staff who answered in 6–8 quarters average 3.68; staff who answered in only 1–2 quarters average 2.40. And would you tell your board engagement is "3.1096"? A 1–5 rating is an ordered label. Counts by level, or the median, are usually more honest than a mean.

The lesson: the AI was careful and still made choices for you. Choosing is your job.

D4

What's the average caseload, and is there a "typical" one?

What is the average caseload?
Break caseload down by role and by full-time vs part-time, for the roles that carry a caseload.

Does a zero mean "none" or "doesn't apply"? Which number would you put in a memo to leadership?

What we found

Across all rows the average is about 14.3. But 1,086 rows are zero because Drivers, Admins, Supervisors, and Intake Coordinators don't carry a caseload. Those zeros mean "doesn't apply." Leave them out and the average is about 21.6. Even that blends very different jobs:

RoleFull-timePart-time
Case Manager32.117.8
Nurse23.213.3
Home Health Aide19.211.1

There is no "typical" caseload. If leadership's real question is "is the workload fair?", the measure they need is caseload by role, adjusted for full-time equivalent. That's an operationalization decision, not an arithmetic one.

D5

The first join, and the trap

Leadership asks whether urban, suburban, or rural sites have more turnover. Turnover lives in one file and site type lives in the other, so we have to combine them.

For each site, show its site_type and its turnover rate: the share of staff_quarters rows with left_org = 1. Explain in plain words how you combined the two files, and show the SQL or the steps.
What would happen if I joined staff_quarters to site_weeks directly on site_id, without first reducing site_weeks to one row per site?

What did the AI do to site_weeks before joining? Why did it need to?

What we found

Gemini first reduced the 832 weekly rows to 8, one per site, and then matched on site_id. That one step is the lesson.

Joined directly, every staff row is copied 104 times, once per week, giving 334,360 rows. The joined table counts 39,624 departures instead of 381. The turnover rate still comes out right (11.9%) only because every row was copied the same number of times. A wrong table that gives some right answers is the one you end up trusting.

Before you join, decide what one row of the result should be.

D6

Audit the data, then audit the audit

Looking at both files, what seems odd, missing, or worth checking before anyone analyzes this data? Check every column, and explain what you think causes each issue.

An audit is a list of claims. Which of these claims have you actually checked?

The one we checked together

Gemini flagged 14 pay decreases. We asked which sites those people moved between, and whether their job level changed. Every one was a staff member who transferred to a lower-paid site at the same level. That's not an error. Riverbend pays differently by site. Odd isn't the same as wrong.

The rest of the AI's list is yours to judge in Part 4 of the Module 1 exercise: some of its explanations hold up, some don't, and it misses something important.

Your turn: the prompt card

About 35 minutes. Work alone or with a partner. Keep your Gemini chat; you will want it for the exercise.

1

What's in here?

What is in each of these files? How many rows and columns does each have?

Check: does it match the data dictionary?

2

What is a row?

What does one row of each file represent? How many different employees are in staff_quarters?

Write one sentence in your own words for each file.

3

What kind of variable, and what's odd?

For each column in site_weeks, tell me which kind it is: labels, ordered labels, yes/no, counts, measurements, or dates and times. Give a one-line reason.

Find one answer you disagree with, or at least question.

4

Your own project

Fill in the two blanks and copy the prompt. Nothing you type here is saved or sent anywhere; it stays in your browser.

Here is a concept from my project.

Choose one measure you would accept and one you would reject, and say why. Rejections are often the more interesting post.

Leave out names and confidential details about your workplace. Gemini with your Willamette account keeps your data out of model training, but your employer's rules still apply.
