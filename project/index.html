The project

From the Project Brief to a video, slide deck, and white paper.

One project, built in stages

A model of some part of an organization, your discipline's lens applied to it, and real data or honest simulation brought to bear on what the model predicts. The organization can be your workplace, a representative organization, or a useful fiction. Simulation is not fabrication: it is a rigorous tradition, and the intellectual work is in designing the model.

The project is built like a research paper, one section per module. Each draft becomes a section of your white paper, so it is not extra work.

Drafts are due on Canvas by 6:00 pm on the day listed. The final project is due by 11:59 pm.

The final deliverable

  1. A video presentation of about 15 minutes. Record yourself presenting your slides; Google Meet or Google Slides recording works. Upload it to Google Drive with your Willamette account and submit the share link on Canvas.
  2. The slide deck you presented.
  3. A white paper of 2–4 pages, written for a decision-maker in your organization: the question, the model, the evidence, your causal claim and its limits, and the decision you recommend, with a forecast of what will happen if it is adopted: a range, and the assumptions behind it.
Honest accounting is required. The strongest projects are not the ones that prove their model right. They are the ones that say clearly what the data establishes, what it cannot, and what it would take to know more.

Your pod

You'll work in a pod of about four classmates from your own location and track. Your pod reads your work first, gives feedback, and hears your dry run. Pods support; they do not co-author. Every project is individual.
