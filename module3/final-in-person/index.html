Honest accounting

"The strongest projects are not the ones that prove their model right. They are the ones that say clearly what the data establishes, what it cannot, and what it would take to know more." That's from the project page, and tonight is about saying it so a decision-maker can't miss it.

Where the limit went: the Columbia slide

In 2003, engineers told NASA managers what they knew about a foam strike on the shuttle Columbia, in a slide. The fact that mattered most, that the foam was far larger than anything that had been tested, was buried in its last bullet. Tufte's point isn't that slides are bad. It's that where you put a limitation decides whether anyone sees it.

Five rules for evidence

RuleIn your white paper
Inform, don't persuadeYour job is to help the decision-maker choose well, not to win.
Balance, not false balanceGive both sides their real weight: not equal time to a weak objection, and not silence on a strong one.
Disclose uncertaintyA range, not a point. Say what drives its width.
State the quality of the evidenceThat's the rung, and the design that got you there.
Pre-empt misunderstandingName the conclusion a reader is most likely to jump to, and say whether it's supported.

Paraphrased from Blastland, Freeman, van der Linden, Marteau, and Spiegelhalter (2020).

Two minutes, alone: open your slides. On which slide does your biggest limitation appear, and where on it? If it's the last bullet of a late slide, move it.

Premortem (15 minutes: 5 alone, 10 together)

Gary Klein's method. Imagine it's a year from now. Your organization adopted your recommendation, and it failed. Write down every reason you can think of, for five minutes, without discussing them. Then a few of you read yours aloud, and we sort them together.

Sort each reason:

  • A limit of your evidence (a rival you couldn't rule out, a rung you couldn't reach) → it goes in the white paper's limits.
  • A condition where it's adopted (a support factor that may be missing, in Cartwright's terms) → it goes in your forecast's assumptions.
  • Something nobody could have known → leave it out, unless it would change the decision.

The dry run (two rounds, with dinner between)

About five live slots a night, signed up in advance: two before dinner and three after. Each live Author presents once, without stopping, at the agreed length. Then about 10 minutes of feedback from the room. Everyone else records the same talk and posts it for their pod, which counts equally. The Skeptic's Chair, in its final form:

RoleJob tonight
AuthorPresents the whole project, as in the video: model, data, claim, limits, forecast
The room, as decision-makerListens as the person the white paper is written for. Afterward: "What would you do on Monday, and how sure are you?"
The room, as SkepticNames the strongest rival explanation left standing, and whether the talk admitted it
The Author, as ScribeWrites down the answers to the four questions below and posts them to the pod thread. The Author gets the last word

The four questions

  1. What is the claim, in one sentence? Does it match what the Author meant?
  2. Which rung does the evidence reach? Did the talk say so?
  3. What would it take to know more, and is it worth it?
  4. What's the forecast, as a range, and what assumption does it rest on?

Recording instead of presenting live? Post the link to your pod's thread and ask two pod-mates to answer the four questions. Listening to someone else's live slot with the four questions in hand is good practice either way.

White paper clinic (25 minutes, at your seat)

Run the Skeptic Gem on your white paper, or swap with whoever is beside you if you like. Read only two things:

  1. The first paragraph. After reading it, could a reader say what decision is being made and what you recommend? If not, rewrite it so they could.
  2. The forecast sentence. Is it a range? Does it say what it assumes? Would a decision-maker know what would make it wrong?

Then, as the decision-maker: "On Monday, I would… because…" If the sentence you get back isn't the recommendation you meant, the paper isn't done.

Your fix list (20 minutes)

From the four questions, the premortem, and the clinic, write the three changes you'll make before Thursday's pre-final version. Start with whatever made a listener misread your claim. Post the list to your pod's thread; your pod will look for those changes on Thursday.
